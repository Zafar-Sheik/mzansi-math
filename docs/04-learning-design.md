# Learning design & study tools

Maths is a *doing* subject. Every lesson is built so learners spend most of their time doing maths, not reading about it.

## The six-step lesson

1. **Hook** – a "Mzansi moment": taxi fares, a viral video, a stadium, a car loan. Why should I care?
2. **See it** – a live graph or visual (drag the parabola, cancel the exponent tiles, watch money compound).
3. **Do it** – worked examples revealed **one step at a time**, then typed-answer practice with hints and full working.
4. **Check it** – a quick quiz with an explanation for every option.
5. **Exam it** – an NSC-style question with a memo.
6. **Celebrate** – XP, streaks and badges.

## Why these tools?

- **Worked examples + self-explanation.** Learners new to a method learn more from studying worked examples than from solving problems cold. Revealing one step at a time, with a "Why?" note and a prompt to predict the next step, turns passive reading into active self-explanation.
- **Retrieval practice.** Quizzes, flashcards and typed practice make learners *pull information out* of memory – the single most effective way to make it stick.
- **Immediate, specific feedback.** The answer checker doesn't just say "wrong": it spots sign errors, missing solutions, unsimplified surds and answers that are "very close" but not exact.
- **Interleaving.** The Quiz Arena and drills mix topics, which feels harder but transfers better to exams.
- **Spaced repetition.** Flashcards use Leitner boxes: cards you know come back later; cards you miss come back tomorrow.
- **Dual coding.** Every algebraic idea is paired with a picture: roots are x-intercepts, inequalities are regions, simultaneous equations are intersections.
- **Endless practice without answer-copying.** Generated questions are new every time, so learners practise the method rather than memorising answers.

## Typed answers

Learners type maths the way they'd write it: `3/4`, `2sqrt(5)` or `2√5`, `x = 2 or x = -3`, `(2; -3)`, `2n^2 + 3n - 1`, `R12 345,67`. Decimal commas and points both work. A live preview shows the typed maths properly formatted. A symbol keypad (√, ^, ±, π…) helps on phones.

## Motivation without pressure

XP rewards *effort* (reading a step, attempting a problem), not just correctness. Wrong answers are always "Not yet". Streaks and badges are visible but never punishing.
