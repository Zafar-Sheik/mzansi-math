# Teacher quick-start (10 minutes)

## 1. Open it

No installs, no sign-up. It runs in any modern browser – including the cheap Android phones many learners have. Progress is stored on each learner's own device.

## 2. Project a tool in class

Open **Toolkit**. Every interactive tool is there, ready for the projector:

- **Function grapher** – drag *a*, *p* and *q* and let the class predict what will happen *before* you move the slider. Switch on **Challenge** for a whole-class "Graph Match" race.
- **Quadratic lab** – show the formula, the discriminant and completing the square updating live as you change *a*, *b* and *c*.
- **Inequality lab** – the clearest way we know to show why "< 0 means between the roots".
- **Money lab** and **Investment timeline** – simple vs compound, depreciation, nominal vs effective, and multi-step timelines.
- **Circle theorem explorer** – drag points round the circle while the class watches the angles stay in proportion. A great lead-in to the proofs.
- **CAST explorer** and **Trig graph lab** – reduction formulae and the effects of a, k, p and q, live.
- **Derivative lab** – shrink h and watch the secant become the tangent: first principles you can *see*.
- **Cubic lab** and **Optimisation lab** – stationary points and concavity live, and "drag to find the biggest box" before the calculus.
- **Annuity lab** – change the rate or the term on a loan and watch the total interest jump. Great for a life-skills conversation.
- **Compound & double angles** – ask the class whether sin(α + β) = sin α + sin β, then test it live. The graph settles the argument.
- **Heights & distances** – turn the 3D view so learners *see* which triangles are horizontal and which are vertical.

## 3. Set a lesson as homework

Every lesson has a link like `/learn/quadratic-equations`. Learners see their progress on each lesson and on the Learn page. Each lesson ends with a quiz; many include an exam-style question with a memo.

## 4. Use the Exam Room

Learners do the working first, then open the memo and tick each mark they earned – exactly how NSC markers work, including follow-on (CA) marks. Use it as self-marked homework or as a revision station.

## 4b. Write a mock paper

**Exam Room → Mock papers** has a full Paper 1 and Paper 2 mock for each grade, and quick mocks of about 50 marks (a new mix every time). Run one in class with the timer, or print the paper and the memo from the start screen. Learners who write online mark their own scripts and get an NSC level and a breakdown by topic – a quick way to see where the class is weak.

## 4c. Print a worksheet

**Teachers → Worksheet generator** makes a printable worksheet from any mix of topics, with answer space and a full memo on a separate page. Copy the link to share it: the same link always gives the same questions. Change the worksheet number for a fresh set (e.g. one per row in a test).

## 5. Use the drills for differentiation

The **Endless drill** in each lesson (and in the Toolkit) generates new questions forever – the strongest learners can chase streaks while you work with a small group. The **Weak-spot coach** on each learner's Progress page tells them which three topics to work on next and gives them a drill aimed at exactly those topics.

## 6. Accessibility in one click

The **Preferences** button (top right) gives each learner: dyslexia-friendly fonts, bigger text, calm mode (no animation), a reading ruler, word anchors, read-aloud speed and focus mode. Show your class where it is on day one.

## 7. Keep an eye on the roadmap

Topics arrive in phases. The Learn page shows what's live and what's coming; *Build roadmap* in this guide lists the plan.
